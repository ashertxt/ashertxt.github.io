UX writing / Concept project

Handshake app 🤝

Handshake is an all-in-one project management app connecting Freelancers and Business Owners. As part of my UX writing certification from UX Content Collective, I reviewed and revised its in-app copy. (This case study features the Freelancer user flow.)

Challenge

Make the experience clear, consistent, and approachable for two different user types, including people who may be low tech.

What I worked on

UX writing

  • Defined brand voice and tone, as well as word usage and style guidelines
  • Created on-brand taglines and headlines
  • Edited UI copy and UX content for two user flows, from sign up to messaging screens

Content design

  • Improved the design of content modules and UI elements in the wireframes
  • Proposed additional functionality to enhance the holistic user experience

Guiding principles

  • Write for both audiences

    Account for the needs of Freelancers and Business Owners.

  • Lower the barrier to entry

    Use familiar language that works for people with different levels of technical experience, including new freelancers.

  • Build consistency

    Establish a shared vocabulary and voice across the experience.

User types and terminology

Handshake simplifies billing and paying tasks for Freelancers and Business Owners. So what words do they use to talk about money? I conducted secondary research to identify terminology unique to each user type, along with words shared in common.

Freelancers

Invite Business Owners to project, provide quotes and payment rates, track hours, request payments

Words used: charge, billable hours, hourly vs. fixed rate, project pricing/per project rate, invoice, log/track hours

Business Owners

Accept invites, write project descriptions, review budgets, oversee progress, send payments

Words used: contract, payroll, billing, one time vs. recurring payment

Shared terminology: negotiation, expectations, requirements, agreement, budget, payment, timeline, project goals, outcomes, scope of work, pay rate, deadline

From research to word choices

These findings informed my terminology guidelines for both user flows. Below are the words I recommended and why.

Approved wordsRationale
Freelancernot self-employed, contractor, or 1099 employeeWe want to keep things simple and “freelancer” is widely recognized.
Business Ownernot CEO or entrepreneur“Business owner” is all encompassing and describes the intended target user.
Time/budget/project trackerinstead of words like “time punch”“Tracker” works with many words, while “time punch” is too specific.
Request, send (payment)not charge, demand, claim, collect“Request” and “send” pair well together and have a neutral connotation.
Pay rate, hourly rate, project budgetnot fixed rate, flat fee, installmentsFreelancers will track hours worked in the app and “rate” is straightforward.
Approve, negotiate (a budget)instead of words like “dispute”“Dispute” sounds negative; the UI copy should encourage users to take action.

Handshake voice/tone guide

Professional, but friendly and conversational

We won't crack jokes, but we're not stone-faced, either. We feel like a long-time, trustworthy business partner (or coworker) who's by your side every step of the way.

Motivational, reassuring

We want our freelancers to feel like they're in control. We'll cheer them on with a “You got this!” attitude, and help them finish project tasks.

We also encourage business owners to be proactive with the platform.

Upbeat, positive

Our users' success and growth is always reason for celebration. We're vocal about how much we care. We may sometimes use expressive emojis in our copy 😉 and show enthusiasm with a well-placed exclamation point.

What if I… design the log in? 🤔

Beyond the project requirements, I mocked up additional screens to explore the returning-user experience and social login options.

Sign up vs. log in

A few notes

UI copy decisions

  • Used “log in” to avoid confusion between sign up and sign in terminology
  • Changed heading to “Nice to see you again” to keep things short and sweet
  • Added “Forgot password?” under the form field because it’s necessary and expected by users

Design decisions

  • Added “Log in” ghost button as secondary action on the welcome screen
  • Suggested “Remember me” functionality so that users don’t have to log in again and again
  • Included “Or log in with” social login options below the traditional form fields

2. Set up

Project info and payment preferences

Payment set up and invite screen

Changes made to set up screens

UI copy edits

  • Changed headings to start with verbs (parallelism) and added short, helpful copy
  • Updated CTAs to “Go back” and “Continue”
  • Revised form field titles and added more instructive placeholder text
  • Changed “+” icon to “+Add” button for clarity

Design and functionality updates

  • Differentiated user flows so that Business Owners create projects while Freelancers join them
  • Added currency selector for possible internationalization of Handshake
  • Redesigned payment preferences screen to show options in large, easy-to-click squares
  • Added “Find an existing member” to the invite screen to account for people already on the app
  • Improved visual interest with illustrations

3. Confirmation message

Changes made to confirmation message

UI copy edits

  • Changed header to “Success!” to motivate users and mark a high point in their journey

More suggestions

  • Consider an “X” in the upper right as another way to close the message (not shown above)

4. Ongoing use

Project overview and hours tracker

Payment tracker and confirm/cancel

Changes made to ongoing use screens

UI copy edits

  • Changed content pattern at top of screens to: [Header]
    [Project name]
  • Added secondary CTA “See All Projects” to every screen since users may have multiple projects
  • Revised subheadings, menu items, and other copy to align with brand voice and agreed upon terms
  • Streamlined formats for dates to “MM/DD/YY” and dollar amounts to “$XX.XX”
  • Included last names across user flows for more specificity and security in billing/paying
  • Updated confirmation and cancellation messages with on-brand copy, better CTAs; added names and dollar amounts for verification
  • Corrected spelling and capitalization errors

Design and functionality updates

  • Added “Approve” and “Negotiate” CTA (new feature) for Business Owners to further discuss hour/budget estimates set by Freelancers
  • Improved content design and organization of the hours tracker page for both user types
    • Added Date, Hours & Rate, and Pay Due titles
    • Changed pay rate format from “X @ $XX” to “X hours at $XX/hr” to add specificity
    • Calculated out Pay Due/daily totals for users to reduce cognitive load

5. Messaging

Messages inbox and details screen

Changes made to messaging screens

UI copy edits

  • Changed content pattern at top of screens to: Messages
    [Project name]
  • Edited subheadings, CTAs, and placeholder text for clarity and consistency
    • For example, changed “Send an answer” to “Write your reply” because “reply” is commonly used
  • Created more obvious “Inbox” content section
    • Added category headings to clearly separate “Unread” vs. “Sent & Received” messages

Design and functionality updates

  • Created mini sender profile above each message with profile pic, name, date, and time stamp
  • Suggested additional functionality for smart responses at the bottom of reply fields
  • Suggested including a search function for messages if the design allows for it

What I learned

This project reinforced that UX writing is much more than “just doing the words.” It requires understanding the intended audience, thinking across the entire product experience, and creating consistent language that helps people navigate each screen and complete their tasks.
